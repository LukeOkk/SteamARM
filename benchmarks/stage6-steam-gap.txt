What is missing between here and the Steam client.
Measured on the running Fedora guest with ftrace, 2026-09-25.

Steam was LIVE for this measurement: the client (pid 923), six web helpers, the
launcher service, all under FEX via binfmt, with FEXServer running. Two 10 s
windows: one system-wide, one filtered to Steam's 11 processes.

HEADLINE: 91 syscalls implemented. Of the 86 distinct syscalls Steam actually
uses, 66 are implemented and 20 are not -- 1.28% of the call volume. That
percentage is misleading. Every one of the 20 is fatal on first use, and
together they are the event loop.

LIVE FD CENSUS in Steam's process tree right now:

  49 anon_inode:[eventfd]      46 anon_inode:[eventpoll]
   4 anon_inode:[signalfd]      2 anon_inode:[timerfd]
   1 inotify                    0 io_uring

The `steam` process alone holds 17 eventfd and 7 epoll descriptors. This is not
the embedded browser: without epoll and eventfd the client does not start.

P0, BLOCKS START-UP:
  epoll_create1/ctl/pwait/pwait2 (20/21/22/441) -- 33334 calls per 10 s
    system-wide, the 4th most frequent syscall on the machine. NO DARWIN
    EQUIVALENT: kqueue is a different model (level vs edge, EPOLLONESHOT,
    EPOLLEXCLUSIVE, epoll nested in epoll), and the resulting fd must be
    waitable by the ppoll() already implemented. This is a table of synthetic
    descriptors over kqueue, not a switch case.
  eventfd2 (19) -- 49 live fds. Coupled to the above: the fd must be pollable.
  futex CMP_REQUEUE/REQUEUE/WAKE_OP -- glibc's pthread_cond_broadcast uses
    CMP_REQUEUE, and futex runs at 50210 calls per 10 s.
  waitid (95), openat2 (437), statx (291) -- structure translation.
  memfd_create (279), execveat (281), rt_sigtimedwait (137) -- FEX itself calls
    these three directly.
  ioctl (29) -- in the enum with no case. The _IOC encoding differs.

P1, VALVE'S OWN IPC: System V semaphores and shared memory. libtier0_s.so
imports semget/semop/semctl, and `ipcs` shows five live semaphore arrays and
shared segments of 10 805 760 bytes created by steamwebhelper. Darwin's default
kern.sysv.shmmax is 4 MiB -- smaller than one of those segments -- and Darwin
has no semtimedop at all.

P2, FD FAMILIES WITH NO EQUIVALENT: inotify (CEF, SDL3 for controller hotplug,
and pressure-vessel-wrap all import it), timerfd, signalfd, mremap, ptrace.

THE BIG DECISION -- pressure-vessel, and it is not deferrable:

  srt-bwrap's undefined symbols: unshare, setns, mount, umount2, capget,
  capset, sethostname, pivot_root. Its strings: CLONE_NEW*, uid_map, gid_map,
  --unshare-user, --add-seccomp-fd, /proc/self/mountinfo, overlayfs.

  macOS has none of it. Not namespaces, not bind mounts, not user namespaces,
  not capabilities.

  ARCHITECTURE_TARGET §4 said "namespaces are implemented only if
  pressure-vessel proves to need them". It needs them, and they cannot be
  implemented. Worse, measured here: Steam's own UI already goes through
  pressure-vessel (pv-adverb 1320 -> steamwebhelper 1346), so this is not a
  problem that waits until a game launches.

  Three ways out, and one must be chosen:
   (a) reimplement bwrap's bind-mount plan as path rewriting inside the runtime
       -- the machinery exists already (translate() + LXRT_ROOT + procfs.c);
   (b) bypass pressure-vessel entirely (STEAM_RUNTIME=0, PRESSURE_VESSEL_*);
   (c) a fake bwrap that only execs its child.

RULED OUT WITH EVIDENCE:
  io_uring -- zero live fds, zero calls in both windows, and no io_uring_*
    symbol in libcef.so, steamwebhelper, steamclient, libtier0_s.so or
    pressure-vessel-wrap. Permanent -ENOSYS is the correct answer.
  rseq -- -ENOSYS is already correct; glibc disables rseq and continues.

/proc BEYOND self: the binaries reference ~40 distinct /proc paths, including
meminfo, cpuinfo, stat, mounts, sys/fs/nr_open, sys/kernel/yama/ptrace_scope,
/proc/<other pid>/status, and /proc/self/ns/*. The static ones are generable;
/proc/self/ns/* and other processes' entries are not.

RECOMMENDED ORDER: epoll+eventfd -> futex CMP_REQUEUE -> waitid/openat2/statx
-> SysV sem+shm -> memfd/execveat/rt_sigtimedwait (these three unblock FEX) ->
wider /proc -> the pressure-vessel decision -> inotify/timerfd/signalfd.

STATUS, 2026-09-25 later: of the 20 missing syscalls, these now exist and are
covered by tests/elf/run.sh against the same binaries on a real Linux kernel:
  epoll/eventfd, futex CMP_REQUEUE/REQUEUE/WAKE_OP, waitid, openat2, statx,
  SysV sem+shm, memfd_create (+ seals on write/pwrite/writev/mmap), execveat,
  rt_sigtimedwait, mremap (22/22), mincore, timerfd (create/settime/gettime),
  signalfd4, inotify (init1/add_watch/rm_watch).
Still absent: ptrace (no plan; Steam's own use is optional), io_uring (correct
-ENOSYS), and the /proc/self/ns/* family, which cannot exist here.
The pressure-vessel decision is taken in MIGRATION_PLAN Stage 6, on the
evidence in stage6-bwrap-plan.txt.
Scores under the runtime vs the same binaries on Linux, 2026-09-25 evening:
mremap 22/22, inotify 41/41, syscall families 26/26, guest-driven W^X 6/6;
timerfd+signalfd 20/23 (ABSTIME arming and readiness through epoll still
wrong -- open). The epoll fix pass left a NULL dereference in EPOLL_CTL_ADD
(lxrt_epoll_ctl, addr 0x19) that killed every glibc test; found by the family
tests within minutes, fixed.
timerfd+signalfd, later: 23/23. Two root causes, both measured: the guest's
CLOCK_MONOTONIC (mach_absolute_time) and Darwin's clock_gettime(MONOTONIC)
are different bases (3.8 s apart here), so an ABSTIME deadline never fired
until every module read the guest's clock through lxrt_guest_clock_ns(); and
kqueue's EVFILT_SIGNAL ignores pthread_kill(), which is how the runtime
delivers self-directed signals, so signal.c now nudges every signalfd whose
mask holds the signal (lxrt_signalfd_notify).
THE PRESSURE-VESSEL DECISION, BUILT (2026-09-25, late): runtime/mounts.c
interprets a bwrap plan -- every option the real plan uses, plus `--args FD`
the way pressure-vessel passes it -- into a fresh sandbox root plus a bind
table carried to children in LXRT_MOUNTS. tests/elf/bwrap_test.c runs one
plan through the real bwrap on Linux (11/11) and through mounts.c under the
runtime (11/11): ro-bind refuses writes with EROFS, bind is writable, symlink
answers readlink, tmpfs is a fresh directory, --ro-bind-data lands the fd's
bytes, --setenv/--unsetenv/--chdir take effect, /proc/self exists. What it
is not: isolation. --unshare-*/--cap-*/--uid/--gid/--seccomp are refused
with ENOSYS and a message, because the evidence says they are never asked.
