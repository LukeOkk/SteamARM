The ID registers, and the bug that made FEX ask for 128 TiB.
Apple M4, macOS 27. 2026-09-24.

FEX reads eleven system registers to decide which instructions it may emit.
Measured from native Mach-O code, Darwin traps most of them:

  CNTVCT_EL0   permitido      TPIDRRO_EL0  permitido
  CNTFRQ_EL0   permitido      DCZID_EL0    permitido
  CNTPCT_EL0   permitido      FPCR         permitido
  CTR_EL0      *** SIGILL *** MIDR_EL1     *** SIGILL ***
  ID_AA64PFR0/1, ID_AA64ISAR0/1/2, ID_AA64MMFR0/1/2/3, ID_AA64ZFR0
                              *** SIGILL, all of them ***

So they are rewritten, like svc and TPIDR_EL0: four instructions that
materialise a 64-bit constant. 23 such sites in FEX, 2 in ld-linux.

THE VALUES ARE REAL, not zeroes. macOS publishes the feature set through
sysctl (hw.optional.arm.FEAT_*), and this core reports FEAT_LSE, AES, PMULL,
SHA1/256/512/3, CRC32, RDM, DotProd, FHM, FlagM2, JSCVT, FCMA, LRCPC2, DPB2,
FRINTTS, SB, BF16, I8MM and more. ID_AA64ISAR0/ISAR1/ISAR2/PFR0/PFR1/MMFR0/MMFR2
are built from them.

The bias is deliberate and one-directional. A feature reported ABSENT costs
performance; a feature reported PRESENT but missing is an illegal instruction
at runtime. Anything sysctl does not confirm is reported absent. Two are
reported absent on purpose even though the hardware has them:

  * Pointer authentication. Apple's implementation is enabled per-process by
    the kernel; a guest that started signing pointers because an ID register
    said it could would fault on the first authenticate.
  * SME. It needs streaming state enabled through SMCR_EL1, which no guest here
    can do.

THE BUG THIS UNCOVERED, and it was in the runtime, not in FEX.

FEX probes the host's VA width by trying to map one page at (1<<Bits)-PAGE_SIZE
for Bits in 57, 52, 48, 47, ... and accepts a width if the mapping succeeds OR
fails with EEXIST. The runtime's MAP_FIXED_NOREPLACE returned EEXIST for every
failure, including "that address does not exist on this machine". FEX therefore
concluded the host had 57-bit addresses, and then -- as it does whenever the
host has 48 bits or more -- tried to reserve [2^47, 2^48) to fence off the
range the guest must never see. Darwin tops out at 2^47, so that could never
succeed, and FEX asserted.

  EEXIST means "busy, try elsewhere".
  ENOMEM means "that address does not exist here".

mach_vm_allocate distinguishes them: KERN_NO_SPACE against KERN_INVALID_ADDRESS.
The runtime now does too, and FEX correctly detects a 47-bit host and skips the
reservation entirely. No patch to FEX was needed -- benchmarks/stage5-va.txt
concluded one was, and that conclusion was wrong.
